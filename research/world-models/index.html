World Models and Simulation for Robot Learning

Research Assistant · Prof. Bo Zhu’s group · Georgia Tech
October 2026–present

My current focus is procedural terrain search: how can we choose simulated environments that reveal weaknesses in navigation? I am reviewing related work and designing initial experiments, with the aim of connecting scenario search to desert simulation and model evaluation.

Current direction ↓Academic overview ↗

A controlled starting point

I plan to begin with a fixed terrain generator, vehicle, and controller, varying a small set of parameters such as slope, surface roughness, and obstacle layout. The proposed loop generates a terrain, runs a navigation trial, records the outcome, and selects the next parameters. Keeping these components fixed would make it easier to study the search strategy itself.

Which scenarios are useful?

Can search find reproducible failures more efficiently than random sampling under the same simulation budget? Does it uncover different failure mechanisms, or repeatedly find the same case? I am considering navigation success, travel time, and failure location as evaluation signals. Parameter bounds should exclude unrealistic terrain, while physical validity and navigability need separate checks: a plausible landscape may still have no traversable route.

The connection to world models

Within Prof. Bo Zhu’s group, RobotGen Studio aims to connect scene reconstruction, physics simulation, task generation, and demonstrations for robot learning, including vision-language-action models. The project also considers how data and experience can be retained and reused across tasks. My terrain-search work is an early step toward studying how these environments can support evaluation and learning.

This direction sits within my broader interest in executable world representations and coding agents for simulation. A terrain generator defines an environment; a predictive world model estimates what actions will do within it. Eventually, scenario search could help identify conditions where prediction errors affect navigation decisions. A vehicle’s failure alone would not establish that its world model was wrong.

Next steps

I plan to compare random sampling with a simple search baseline and retain replayable cases for analysis. The next stage would extend the group’s desert simulator and integrate scenario search into navigation and model testing. Simulation fidelity, search efficiency, and scenario diversity will guide how I refine the research question.
