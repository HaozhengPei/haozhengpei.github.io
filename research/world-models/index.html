Executable world models

Research Assistant · Prof. Bo Zhu’s group · Georgia Tech

I am exploring how coding agents can construct and revise executable representations of a world. My current focus is on diagnosing errors across representations and preserving state when the world’s rules are intentionally changed.

Current work

This work is at the literature-review and problem-formulation stage. I am comparing approaches that use code to represent world state, execute dynamics, and guide visual generation. I am particularly interested in the interface between a running program, a proxy representation of its state, and the generated video. The questions below guide this exploration.

Where did an error begin?

When a generated video violates a world’s rules, did the program produce the wrong state, did the proxy lose necessary information, or did the video depart from its conditions? I want to investigate which observations distinguish these causes, and when additional evidence is needed before choosing a parameter update or a program revision.

What should survive a rule change?

An intentional change should alter future behaviour without accidentally erasing existing objects, identities, or event history. I am considering how to specify what must change and what must remain, then distinguish intended effects from regressions. A related question is how to identify plans whose assumptions no longer hold after the update.

Next steps

The next step is to define small, controlled tests with observable intermediate states and explicit rule changes. These could help compare diagnosis and revision strategies before extending the scope.
