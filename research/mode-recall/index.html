Certifying What Your Generative Model Covers: Labeller-Conditional, Finite-Sample Mode-Recall Guarantees

First-author work · ICLR 2027, under review

A generator can produce convincing images while rarely producing some classes or attribute combinations. I study how finite samples can support reliable conclusions about that coverage, even when the classifier used to label samples makes mistakes.

Manuscript (PDF) ↗ Selected results ↓

The question

Has a model omitted a mode, or have we simply not sampled enough? A low coverage estimate alone cannot distinguish these possibilities. Feature-space recall can also conflate image fidelity with mode coverage.

The method

For a chosen frequency threshold, the procedure classifies each mode as at or above threshold, below threshold, or inconclusive. Independent sample splits and confusion-aware thresholds account for labelling errors. A robust linear-programming variant works with a joint confidence set for classifier confusion and mode frequencies.

The certified bound never exceeded true coverage in all 120 trials across ten removal levels. These experiments check the method’s behaviour; finite-sample validity comes from the analysis. Further experiments cover DCGANs, rectified flow, pretrained diffusion and attribute-defined subgroups.

My contribution

My work includes developing the certification framework, proving validity and consistency results, deriving rare-mode sample-complexity bounds, and conducting experiments across generator families. Consistency is established for the LP variant; the default deconvolved rule remains valid but can be conservative.

What the guarantee covers

The guarantee depends on classifier-confusion bounds holding on generated samples. It concerns frequencies of labeller-defined subgroups, not diversity within each subgroup. Rare modes and sparse calibration can leave decisions inconclusive. A human audit of generated CelebA-HQ faces found that classifier transfer held for some attributes and failed for others, making this assumption an explicit part of the evaluation.
