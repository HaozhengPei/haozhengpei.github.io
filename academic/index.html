ICLR 2027 Under reviewMay 2026–present

Certifying What Your Generative Model Covers

Labeller-Conditional, Finite-Sample Mode-Recall Guarantees

Haozheng Pei1, Jian Xu2

1 Georgia Institute of Technology · 2 RIKEN

First author

A finite-sample framework that certifies whether each predefined mode has probability at or above a tolerance, below it, or leaves the result inconclusive. It accounts for imperfect classifier labels, conditional on the labeller’s confusion bounds holding on generated data.

I established validity guarantees and consistency for the linear-programming variant, with sample-complexity bounds for rare-mode certification, and evaluated GANs, flow matching, and pretrained diffusion models on StackedMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA subgroups.

OpenReview ↗Paper PDF ↗Research overview ↗
Results & scope +

On 1,000-mode StackedMNIST, two DCGAN capacities and a rectified-flow model yielded certified coverage of 29%, 53%, and 73%, against true coverage of 54%, 92%, and 100%; nearest-neighbor recall was near zero for all three. These certificates are conservative lower bounds, not point estimates. The work measures coverage across predefined modes, not diversity within a mode.

Clink! Chop! Thud!
Learning Object Sounds from Real-World Interactions

Mengyu Yang, Yiming Chen, Haozheng Pei, Siddhant Agarwal, Arun Balajee Vasudevan, James Hays

Third author · Georgia Tech

Learning which object produces an interaction sound in real-world egocentric video. The ICCV model achieves 49.6% and 43.9% top-1 sounding-object detection accuracy on EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D, respectively.

I contributed annotation and feature pipelines for object-level supervision, and ran evaluations and ablations on EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D.

World Models and Simulation for Robot Learning

Research Assistant · Prof. Bo Zhu’s group · Georgia Tech

I work with Prof. Bo Zhu’s group on RobotGen Studio, exploring world models and simulation for robot learning. My work combines literature review with early experiment design: how executable world representations and controllable environments can help us test a model or navigation system under changes in its surroundings.

My current focus is procedural terrain parameter search. I am studying how to vary slope, surface roughness, and obstacle spacing within reasonable ranges to identify reproducible navigation failures, and how to compare search strategies under the same simulation budget. I am designing initial tests in a simpler environment, with plans to integrate this search workflow into the group’s desert simulator and extend it as the simulator develops.

Georgia Tech Research projectAug 2025–Aug 2026

Probabilistic Forecasting for
Wind Generation and Power Markets

Probabilistic wind and solar forecasts that connect uncertainty in renewable generation to imbalance pricing, dispatch, and price formation.

I worked with domain-informed Transformer and DKFormer models, ARIMA and SVM baselines, and spatiotemporal features from turbine and weather data covering more than 10,000 assets. The work used ensemble learning and Bayesian model combination to combine forecasts.
